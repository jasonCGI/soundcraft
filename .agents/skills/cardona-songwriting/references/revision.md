# Revision and audit

Preserve the song's identity while improving the listener's experience.

## Diagnose before rewriting

Check these dimensions and cite lines or sections as evidence:

1. **Promise:** Can a listener state what the song is about without relying only on the title?
2. **Hook:** Is the most memorable phrase also the emotional center?
3. **Progression:** Does each repeated section change context, intensity, or meaning?
4. **Specificity:** Which details could be photographed or heard? Which phrases could belong to any song?
5. **Prosody:** Do natural word stresses fit the likely musical stresses? Mark estimates when no melody is supplied.
6. **Line shape:** Do adjacent lines create useful rhythmic consistency? Are deviations intentional?
7. **Rhyme:** Does rhyme add momentum, or does it force predictable vocabulary and inverted syntax?
8. **Voice:** Are point of view, tense, diction, and emotional distance coherent?
9. **Performance:** Can the requested lead and backing parts breathe, enter, and respond naturally?

## Revision order

Repair the song promise and hook before polishing individual rhymes. Then fix section jobs, narrative progression, line shape, sound, and word choice. Surface edits cannot rescue a chorus that delivers the wrong idea.

For every proposed replacement, say what problem it solves. Offer two or three materially different options when the choice is aesthetic rather than corrective.

## Common machine-written signals

Treat these as prompts for inspection, not automatic failures:

- abstract emotion without an observable action;
- symmetrical sections that never surprise;
- every pair of lines ending in an obvious rhyme;
- inflated words where ordinary speech would be stronger;
- familiar weather, fire, darkness, heartbeat, or broken-glass imagery with no song-specific meaning;
- a chorus that explains the verse instead of transforming it;
- backing vocals that merely repeat every lead line.

Do not remove a familiar image that the user chose deliberately. Make it specific to this speaker and moment.
